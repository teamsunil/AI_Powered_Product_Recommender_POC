Thermal or night vision?

The short version our assistant uses when you ask about the difference.

Thermal imaging

Reads heat rather than light. Warm animals, people and engines stand out against cooler surroundings in total darkness, light fog or scrub.

  • Best for finding things: scanning paddocks, fence lines and bush edges.
  • Works day and night with no light source needed.
  • Shows shapes and heat, not colour or fine surface detail.
  • Higher sensor resolution (for example 384 × 288 over 256 × 192) and lower NETD give more detail at distance.

Digital night vision

A sensitive camera that uses available light plus an infrared (IR) illuminator. It shows terrain and detail more naturally, and many models give full-colour images by day.

  • Best for identifying things at closer range and for day-and-night use.
  • Night range depends on the IR illuminator; for example, the Heimdal H4D is listed with up to 200 m detection.
  • Usually lower cost than thermal for the same build quality.
  • Can't see through scrub or detect heat signatures.
